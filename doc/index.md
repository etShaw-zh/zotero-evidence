# Zotero Evidence

Zotero Evidence helps people conducting evidence reviews turn papers into a
structured dataset. It works inside Zotero and keeps each extracted value tied
to supporting text in the source paper, so you can inspect how a conclusion was
reached.

It is intended for researchers, students, librarians, and review teams who want
a guided workflow for screening studies and extracting evidence. You do not
need to be a programmer.

## How the workflow fits together

1. Import search results into an Evidence project. Duplicate records are
   identified during this source-import path.
2. Define the review question and inclusion and exclusion criteria.
3. Review title-and-abstract (TA) screening suggestions and make the final
   decision yourself.
4. Review full-text (FT) criterion checks and make the final decision yourself.
5. Define a Codebook, review suggested values and quotations, and confirm the
   evidence you want to keep.
6. Optionally synthesize confirmed evidence, export review data, and archive
   the project as a `.zip` for backup or sharing.

The central principles are traceability, human confirmation, and working with
your Zotero libraries. AI output is a suggestion, not a final screening or
coding decision. Project collections and records remain associated with the
Zotero library in which you create the project; calls to an AI provider are an
explicit external data transfer.

## Start here

- [Install Zotero Evidence](getting-started/installation.md)
- [Create your first project](getting-started/first-project.md)
- [Configure an AI provider](getting-started/ai-provider-setup.md) (optional,
  for AI-assisted steps)

**Check AI output.** AI services can make mistakes, omit relevant evidence, or
produce plausible but unsupported text. Review every suggestion against the
original paper before confirming it. Do not send confidential or restricted
material to a provider unless its terms and your research approvals permit it.
